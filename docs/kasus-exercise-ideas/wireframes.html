1 · Die Pronomen-Brücke

Cross-language bridge · English pronouns → German articles
✕Pronomen-Brücke?

You already say this in English:

I see him — never “I see he”.

German does the same thing, to every noun:

Ich sehe ____ Hund.

m der Hund

der
den ✓
dem

Richtig · Akkusativ

him = den. The “m” on hi-m is the same “n” on de-n. English only bothers on pronouns; German bothers everywhere.

Weiter
The hook: English has cases — he/him/his, I/me/my — hidden in pronouns. Rounds start on pronouns (er/ihn/ihm), where intuition is instant, then swap in articles on the same sentence frames. Three-stage ramp: he/him → er/ihn → der/den.
Reuses: KasusOptionGrid, KasusFeedback header, GenderTag, EndingsFrame sentence data.

2 · Wer handelt? · Who acts?

Minimal pairs · read endings for meaning
✕Wer handelt?

Den Hund beißt der Mann.

Who does the biting?

🐕 der Hund
🧍 der Mann ✓

Genau!

Word order can flip in German — the endings cannot. der marks the doer (Nom), den the one who gets it (Akk). English needs word order for this; German does not.

Weiter
The hook: no blank to fill — comprehension instead of production. Pairs like Der Hund beißt den Mann / Den Hund beißt der Mann teach that case is the meaning, the exact skill real reading needs. Chinese speakers get a bonus framing: this is what 把 buys you — role marking that frees word order.
Reuses: KasusProgressStrip, KasusFeedback, KasusRich case-color markup.

🥁 Der Rhythmus

der·die·das·die / den·die·das·die / dem·der·dem·den / des·der·des·der

Say it as four beats. The whole table is one line of music — rese·nese·mrmn·srsr with the sound attached.

The hooks: (a) a particle lens — each cell gets a plain-English meaning label (the / acts-on / to / of). For learners who read Mandarin there is an optional overlay: 把/给/的 are the Chinese particles that mark the same roles, so the German ending reads as a particle glued onto the article. (b) a rhythm lens — the table as four chantable beats, optional haptic tap per beat. Plus the honest headline: only der flinches — 75% of Akkusativ is identical to Nominativ.
Reuses: CaseEndingsTable with a third cell line, codeText coloring, sensoryFeedback haptics.
