Die vier Fragen auf Englisch

An English sentence, one part of it ringed, and the four German case questions as the answer buttons. Then the same job in German. Drawn in the app's existing visual language (progress strip, option grid, feedback card, case colors) so it sits next to Rollen and the Pronomen-Brücke without looking foreign. Every sentence on this page is a shipped item with its own English twin, from Resources/kasus_concepts.json (and one from verb_prepositions.json). Three scaffolding presets are drawn further down, under Stufen. The write-up, the measured pool sizes and the limits are in ENGLISH_ANCHORS.md next to this file.

A3 · Wessen? and the flip

The one English case that survived
✕Welche Frage??
EnglischBeideDeutsch

Englisch

The student takes the friend's jacket after school.

Deutsch

Der Schüler nimmt die Jacke des Freundes nach der Schule.

GenitivWessen? ✓whose

Richtig · Genitiv

Gehört zu (hangs on another noun) → Genitiv. Whose jacket? The friend's. Whose is the one English question word that still carries a case ending.

English puts the owner first („the friend's jacket“), German puts it second („die Jacke des Freundes“). Same case, opposite order.

Weiter
The hook: the Genitiv is the one case an English speaker owns already, twice over („the man's car“ and „the car of the man“). Showing the two lines together makes the word-order flip the lesson, instead of a surprise in the stories later. Careful: English „of“ is not reliably a Genitiv („a friend of mine“ → von mir, Dativ), so of-phrases only get asked when the item's target really is a Genitiv attribute.
Reuses: KasusConceptFacts (the role is read off the target's reason), the Merkhilfe „Genitiv = in the genes“.

Präposition → the preposition picks the case, and no question gets asked. wegen always takes the Genitiv. That is Kasus-Check question one, before any of the four.

B2 · the role inverters

gefallen · gehören · schmecken · the subject swaps sides
✕Die englische Falle?

Englisch

My mother likes the film.

In the German sentence, which part is the subject?

My mother
the film ✓

Umgedreht · flipped

Der Film gefällt meiner Mutter.

The film does the pleasing, so the film is the Nominativ and the person is Dativ. English says „my mother likes the film“ and hides the whole structure.

Same flip: gehören (the book belongs to her), schmecken (the soup tastes good to him), fehlen (I miss you).

Weiter
Why this is the hardest item on the page: it breaks „find the English doer → Nominativ“ at the root, not at the edge. So it asks a different question (which part is the subject?) rather than pretending the four anchors apply. Four verbs, high frequency, worth their own round.
Reuses: the Dativ lesson card „Die Dativ-Verben“ (it already teaches this flip), EndingsFrame's gefallen/gehören frames.

English „wait for“ does not become German für. warten comes with auf + Akkusativ and the pair has to be learned whole.

highlighter and tag.fill are not new: they are Markieren's and Rollen's own symbols from the registry, so a switch wears the glyph of the thing it turns on. slider.horizontal.3 and play.fill are the deck launcher's. The glyphs drawn here are SVG stand-ins; check every name in SF Symbols before it ships, the way a What's New symbol gets checked.

Presets, not a matrix. Six switches is 64 states to reason about and to pin with golden numbers; three named presets is three, and the switches stay for whoever wants them. Same shape as KasusRolesMode, which also locks once an answer is in, so a setting can't be changed mid-item to rescue a guess.
Why the sheet's rows get a bare glyph and not a chip: a settings row is neither a task nor a place, and a 34pt tinted chip on six rows in a row reads as six doors. A 17pt glyph in the secondary colour is list adornment, which is what iOS does in Settings and what these rows are.
Per kind, not per screen: which switches a kind shows is a row in KasusDrillMeta, so the next exercise inherits the sheet instead of growing its own.

Was dein Level vorschlägt

A1 → Leicht    A2 → Leicht
B1 → Normal   B2 · C1 → Frei

A first guess, not a finding. CEFR level says what you can say; it does not say whether anyone ever taught you what a direct object is, which is the whole reason Verstehen exists. A B2 who never met the terms turns the help back on, and that has to be one tap, not a hunt.

Nach der ersten Runde

The round itself is the better signal. After a Leicht round that comes out near-perfect, the next launch offers Normal once as a row the learner can ignore. The number that counts as „near-perfect“ is not in this note on purpose: it gets measured on real rounds first, the way the coach's shaky threshold was.

Three states, one of them silent: automatisch (derived from germanLevel on every render, nothing stored, so changing your level in Settings moves it), von dir gewählt (stored the moment a switch or a preset is touched, and level changes no longer reach it), and „Wieder automatisch“ to go back. Exactly the contract germanLevel already has: „exercises seed from it and never write back, so running one story at B2 doesn't quietly make you a B2 learner everywhere“ (MLXModelManager).
So the first run of every exercise is already configured and the setup screen is something a learner can go their whole time without opening.

Why the buttons follow the unit, not the sentence

Askable spans per item: 2 on 457 items, 3 on 148, 4 on 44. Distinct cases per item: exactly two on 483 of the 700, one on 52, three or more on 165.

So „show only the cases this sentence contains“ turns 69% of the pool into a coin flip, and hands the answer over outright on the 52 single-case items. Cutting the buttons to the unit's cases in play does the same job honestly: Nominativ shows one, Akkusativ two, Dativ three, Genitiv and Alle Fälle the lot. The path is already the difficulty curve.

It also sets the ceiling on „ganzer Satz“: a 2-span sentence is one real ask plus one elimination, which is why Frei asks a single phrase.

The one data gap: Keine Frage is thin on Nominativ (1) and Dativ (10), because 249 of the 416 preposition-governed targets have no en span. The English text already contains the phrase („in the forest“, „for the man“); only the span is unmarked. A one-off script can propose them by locating the phrase in the English line, then a hand read. No LLM round, no new sentences.

Where it cannot work

The answer to „can this do all English sentences?“

No. Five systematic classes, not edge cases

  • Dativ verbs. „helps the brother“ reads as a direct object. 206 spans.
  • Role inverters. gefallen, gehören, schmecken, fehlen: the English subject becomes the German Dativ.
  • Prepositions. „wait for“ → auf + Akk; „a friend of mine“ → von mir, Dativ, not Genitiv.
  • English has no form to ask with. him/her/them covers Akkusativ and Dativ both; whom is dead in speech; „It's me“ is accusative where German is nominative.
  • No counterpart at all. „every day“ → jeden Tag (bare Akkusativ); „He was given a book“ → Ihm wurde ein Buch gegeben.

And the deeper reason

The case is a property of the German sentence. KasusValidator needs German text with located targets to prove anything, so for a sentence the learner types in English there is nothing to validate until a German twin exists. The tutor seam could write one (KasusConceptBank.merging, unused today), but the documented harvest is about a third of planned phrases, and a wrongly highlighted span teaches a wrong case.

So: authored pairs only. Which is fine, because 1224 of them already ship.

The design that survives this: don't aim at „any English sentence“. Aim at two bounded pools. Exercise A only ever asks spans where the question has an answer, and its fifth button teaches that sometimes it doesn't. Exercise B is stocked from precisely the spans A refuses, and its whole lesson is your English is lying to you here, use the list. The 23% that breaks the idea becomes the second exercise instead of a bug report.
